# Resources

Curated pointers for going deeper: where the community actually gathers, where to see finished
builds for inspiration, and where to buy a starting point instead of building every part from
raw components.

## Communities

- **r/cyberDeck** (Reddit) - the largest general cyberdeck community, tens of thousands of
  members, several new build logs posted daily. Good for broad exposure to build variety;
  reported by some builders as less consistently welcoming to newcomers than the Cyberdeck Cafe
  Discord below.
- **r/writerDeck** (Reddit) - the writerdeck-focused counterpart, smaller and more tightly
  scoped to distraction-free writing builds.
- **Cyberdeck Cafe** (cyberdeck.cafe) and its **Discord server** - a large, active,
  explicitly newcomer-friendly community with thousands of members, a build gallery, and its
  own practical [build guide](https://cyberdeck.cafe/build). A good first stop if you want
  real-time help while planning or troubleshooting a build.
- **Cyberpunk Forums** - a longer-running cyberpunk-culture forum with an active cyberdeck
  sub-community, useful for the fiction/aesthetic side of the hobby as well as builds.

## Build Galleries and Reference Sites

- **Hackaday.io** - the most consistently deep source of documented, real-world builds, and
  home of the annual/recurring Cyberdeck Contest (running since 2022), which is one of the best
  single places to see a large number of high-quality, fully documented builds side by side.
- **[awesome-cyberdeck](https://github.com/DayZedAndConfused762/awesome-cyberdeck)** (GitHub) -
  a community-maintained curated link list covering notable builds, components, guides,
  operating systems, and software, organized specifically for people planning their own build.
  A good single starting bookmark before doing your own deeper research.
- **writerDeck.org** - the reference site for the writerdeck sub-community specifically,
  including pointers to writerdeck-focused open-source software like WareWoolf and ZeroWriter.

## Video

Search "cyberdeck build" directly on YouTube for the most current build-log and tutorial
content; the field moves fast enough that any specific channel list here would go stale quickly.
As reference points at time of writing, both dedicated build-walkthrough videos (e.g., full
step-by-step tutorials for specific named builds like the Pi Flux cyberdeck) and roundup videos
covering several builds at once (e.g., "Brilliant Cyberdecks using Raspberry Pi"-style
compilations) are common and useful formats. Hackaday's own YouTube channel also periodically
covers standout Cyberdeck Contest entries.

## Blogs and Write-Ups

- **Bystroushaak's blog** (blog.rfox.eu) - a detailed, opinionated writeup on cyberdeck design
  philosophy and component tradeoffs, worth reading alongside this guide's
  [Build Guide](04-build-guide.md) for a second perspective.
- **Hackster.io's coverage of Jay Doscher's builds** - a good way to see the movement's most
  influential builder's work and design philosophy directly (see [History](02-history.md)).
- Individual build blogs turn up constantly via the communities above; following a few builders
  whose use case matches yours is often more useful than any single reference site.

## Books and Longer-Form Reading

- **Neuromancer** by William Gibson (1984) - the novel that coined the term; essential
  background for the aesthetic and conceptual roots of the hobby, not a technical reference.
  See [History](02-history.md).
- **Cyberpunk 2020 / Cyberpunk RED** (R. Talsorian Games) and **Shadowrun** (FASA/Catalyst) -
  the tabletop RPGs that gave fictional decks their most detailed treatment after Gibson; useful
  for aesthetic and world-building inspiration for a themed build.
- A small number of independently published practical cyberdeck-building guides exist (search
  current listings on Amazon/Kindle or similar storefronts for "cyberdeck build guide" - specific
  titles and editions turn over often enough that this document won't name one directly); vet
  any purchased guide against the free community resources above before relying on it, since
  quality varies a lot in this self-published niche.

## Kits and Starting Points

Building every component from scratch is not required, buying a partially- or fully-assembled
base and customizing from there is a completely normal way into the hobby, and often a good
choice for a first build:

- **Pre-designed cyberdeck bodies/shells** exist that accept a specific compute module (for
  example, bodies designed around the Raspberry Pi Compute Module line) and handle the
  keyboard, display, and enclosure integration for you, leaving customization for later builds
  once you understand the tradeoffs firsthand.
- **The ClockworkPi uConsole** is a widely-cited example of a commercially available,
  fully-assembled handheld that the community treats as a legitimate "ready-made cyberdeck" or
  a strong base for further modification: a swappable compute-module system (supporting several
  ARM and RISC-V module options), a 5-inch IPS display, integrated thumb keyboard and trackball,
  and dual 18650 battery power, all in a pocketable handheld shell.
- **Marketplace sellers** (Etsy and similar) offer both complete small decks and individual
  enclosure/case components, useful for sourcing a single missing piece rather than building
  everything yourself.
- Buy a kit or pre-built base only after you've read the [Build Guide](04-build-guide.md) and
  know roughly what tradeoffs you want, so you can judge whether a given kit's fixed choices
  (its compute module, display size, keyboard layout) actually fit your use case.

## Events

- **Hackaday's Cyberdeck Contest** - the hobby's most visible recurring showcase event, run
  since 2022 with hundreds of entries in some years; a good target to build toward if you want
  a deadline and an audience for a first serious build.
- **Ham radio field days and similar amateur radio events** are a natural venue for
  radio-focused decks specifically, since the equipment and the event culture were already
  aligned before the cyberdeck hobby existed (see [Modern Usage](03-modern-usage.md)).
